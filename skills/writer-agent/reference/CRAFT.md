# CRAFT.md — the trainable writing-craft weight

Format-agnostic. What makes a stranger read the next sentence, how a lead
is built, how evidence is handled, what earns a paragraph its place. No
publication mechanics, no schedules, no platform quirks.

## Lead

- The lead is the sentence doing real work: a fact, a stake, or a number the
  reader could not produce themselves. Cut everything written before it.
- Open on an observable, concrete scene — a time, an action, a failure —
  never an abstract claim or a definition.
- First person is an instrument pointing at the reader's problem, not the
  subject the reader is asked to care about.

## Structure

- Order the piece by the reader's priority, never by research chronology.
- State the promise up front in a plain sentence.
- Each section stands alone; restate the needed point fresh in one line.
- One claim, one place. Do not repeat the same point, citation, or call to
  action in a second spot.
- Show gradations and spectrums; a flattened single-point claim hides the
  real shape of the thing.

## Evidence

- Every claim carries Claim, Evidence (the actual source), and Status.
- Use primary sources and name who made the thing on first mention.
- A mechanism claim answers who, where, how, what governs it, proof it is
  real, and its honest limit.
- State the actual scale: runs, duration, model, and earned amount — verified,
  not estimated.

## Voice

- Cut filler openings, hedge phrases, and false agency.
- Replace an adjective with the number it stands in for.
- Write the journey: what was tried, what happened, and what it earned.
