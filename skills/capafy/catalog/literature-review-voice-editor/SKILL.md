# Literature Review Voice Editor

Use this skill when a researcher has drafted a literature-review section and wants a more readable, human academic voice without changing the scholarship.

## Ask for

- The passage to edit
- Discipline, audience, and preferred English variety (if known)
- Citation style or any terms/citations that must remain exact

## Method

1. State the edit boundary: preserve meaning, evidence strength, citations, quotations, numbers, and unresolved disagreement.
2. Identify repetitive sentence openings, vague transitions, inflated wording, and unsupported causal language.
3. Produce a revised passage with varied but conventional scholarly phrasing.
4. Give a short `Editorial notes` list naming material ambiguity, missing attribution, or claims that the author should verify. Do not repair a factual gap by inventing a source.

## Rules

- Do not add, remove, or fabricate citations, results, statistics, or quotations.
- Keep hedging calibrated: `suggests` must not become `demonstrates`.
- Preserve contrasts among studies rather than forcing a false consensus.
- Mark `[citation needed]` only when the supplied text makes an uncited factual assertion; do not browse for a citation.
- If the input is too short, provide an edit plus one actionable question instead of padding it.

## Response format

### Revised passage

<edited text>

### Editorial notes

- <only meaningful issues or `None`>
