Affirmation app alternative — Anicca uses your own voice

no celebrity narrators · no streak gamification · 10% revenue → basic income

If you've tried I AM, Mantra, Affirmations, or YouTube affirmation videos and bounced off — there's a structural reason. The voice in those apps is not yours. It's Andy Puddicombe's, or Jennifer Lopez's, or a stock narrator hired by the app to sound calm. The cognitive distance between "calm-stranger-on-recording" and "me-in-my-actual-life" is wider than these apps acknowledge.

Anicca is an affirmation app built on one observation: the inner critic is loud, and the inner ally is quiet. The fastest way to amplify the inner ally is to give it your own voice.

How Anicca works

  1. Open the app. Tap "Speak."
  2. Say the words you wish you heard. Could be "I notice without judging." Could be "this too will pass."
  3. Anicca records and plays back to you — in your own voice — daily at the times you set.

That's the whole app.

The Buddhist root (not religious)

Anicca is a Pali word — Pali is the original language of Theravada Buddhism. It means impermanence. The Buddha taught that everything conditioned arises and passes. Anxiety arises and passes. Loud thoughts arise and pass. Your inner critic is loud — but it is not permanent.

The app does not preach Buddhism. The word is there as a quiet anchor: a reminder that the affirmation you speak today will land differently in three weeks. That's not failure. That's the practice.
