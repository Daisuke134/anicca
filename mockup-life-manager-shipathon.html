The four answers

What are we building?

An iPhone version of the existing Life Manager: the same calendar, context, travel, call, and notification loops that already run through Telegram.

WHAT APP?
Life Manager for iPhone

A whole-life manager that connects to Google Calendar and uses the context around your day to decide what needs to happen next.

Not: a passive calendar or a chat assistant waiting for a question.

FOR WHO?
People who want their life managed in context

People whose days contain real commitments, travel, routines, calls, messages, and places — and who want an agent to carry the coordination burden.

Example: a person moving between home, work, school, appointments, and events.

WHAT PROBLEM?
Life is scattered across too many systems

Calendar knows the event. Maps knows a route. Gmail may contain the address. Your phone knows where you are. You still have to connect all of that, decide when to leave, and act on the reminder.

Failure: the event starts at 14:00, but the person notices the travel problem at 13:55.

SOLVE HOW?
Life Manager turns context into action

It reads Calendar, remembers places, resolves unknown locations, calculates a door-to-door route, writes travel into the day, calls at T-15/T-10/T-5, and can notify people if the user is late.

iPhone: the same manager is visible in Today, Route, Context, and Chat.

The product thesis: the iPhone is not a smaller version of a route assistant. It is the native surface where the existing Life Manager manages the user's day.

The promise

“I do not have to coordinate my life across five apps. Life Manager already did it.”

The difference

The route is executable.

A route is only useful when a person can follow it without opening another app and interpreting it again.

NOT A SUGGESTION
Exact station entry

“Walk to Shibuya Station A2 entrance.” The manager gives the gate or exit that matters for this destination.

NOT A LABEL
Exact train

“Board the Tokyu Toyoko Line express bound for Motomachi-Chukagai.” The user knows what to board.

NOT A MAP LINK
Exact transfer path

Station, line, direction, transfer, and final exit are written in the conversation as an action sequence.

NOT A WEAK ALERT
Forceful intervention

The manager calls at T-15, T-10, and T-5. If the person is late, it can calculate arrival and send the late notice.

What the prototype proves

One product, one mobile channel.

The backend remains Life Manager. This shipathon prototype shows how the existing Telegram experience becomes an iPhone app without shrinking the ambition.

Google Calendar → Life Manager core → iPhone.
Calendar events, context resolution, places memory, transit planning, wake calls, chat replies, and late recovery stay part of one manager. The iPhone adds Today, route detail, context, and chat as a first-class surface.
