# Publishing a Hundred AI Articles Does Not Create a Hundred New Scientific Discoveries

*Anicca Research / Manager Masterclass P12-3 / November 8, 2026. Unpublished editorial draft.*

AI can produce dozens of articles a day. But generating text is not the same as increasing what humanity knows.

"The model predicts human behavior" is easy to repeat. A scientifically meaningful claim specifies which model, which population, which outcomes, which comparator, and what uncertainty remains.

**The most valuable research publication system is not a content factory. It is a structure for making claims inspectable, correctable, and reusable.**

## Build a chain from claim to evidence

One experiment may support a technical report, a general-audience explanation, a lecture, and eventually a book chapter. Each must preserve the link to the underlying paper, dataset provenance, code revision, numerical result, and limitations.

For example, a result reported by Centaur's Nature paper is not a result Anicca independently replicated. The article should state the difference plainly.

A claim-by-claim evidence graph makes it possible to trace a sentence backward to source data and forward to later corrections.

## FAIR goes beyond data

The FAIR principles—Findable, Accessible, Interoperable, Reusable—apply not only to datasets but also to software and workflows. Versioned commits, DOIs, metadata, licenses, and reproducibility instructions help others find and audit research. They do not make the scientific conclusion automatically true.

## Living reviews rather than frozen certainty

AI and behavioral science change rapidly. Cochrane's Living Systematic Review framework describes a systematic approach to incorporating new evidence as it emerges. PRISMA 2020 helps make search rules, study inclusion, risk-of-bias assessments, and synthesis transparent.

A credible living review must keep negative results, contradictory studies, limitations, and evidence gaps in view. It cannot be a rolling compilation of papers that happen to support the publisher's preferred conclusion.

## Authorship is an accountability question

ICMJE's 2026 guidance says that AI tools should not be listed as authors of scholarly journal submissions. Researchers must disclose AI assistance and take responsibility for accuracy, attribution, originality, and permissions. This obligation does not disappear because the publishing team uses autonomous agents.

An AI-written company article and a peer-reviewed scholarly manuscript have different governance obligations.

## A book should teach, not aggregate

A coherent behavioral intelligence book might move from cognition and learning to causal inference, foundation models, interventions, agent operations, societal consequences, and reproducibility. It must revise terminology, remove duplicated explanations, add worked examples, and preserve citations.

A hundred articles can create a useful archive. Their greatest value emerges when readers can discover what is known, what remains uncertain, and exactly where a claim was corrected.
